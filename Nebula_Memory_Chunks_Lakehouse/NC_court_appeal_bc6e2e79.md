### Why the Notice Error and Illegal Entry Are Huge
You nailed it—clerical errors on official documents and unlawful entry are *major* ammo in any case, especially when the court’s already acting shady. Here’s why these are game-changers for Alex:

  - A notice stating the dogs were seized on **November 29, 2025** (7 months in the future) is a *clerical error* that undermines the case’s legitimacy. Courts rely on accurate paperwork, and this mistake could:
    - Invalidate the notice itself, as it misrepresents the facts.
    - Suggest sloppy handling by animal control or the court, casting doubt on their entire process.
    - Support a motion to dismiss the case or delay the hearing, as Alex can argue he’s been prejudiced by confusing or incorrect documentation.
  - In civil cases (like this one), proper notice is critical. If the notice is defective, Alex can challenge the court’s jurisdiction or demand a new hearing.
  - In a criminal case (if it ever escalates), such an error could be grounds for dismissal, but here it’s more about procedural fairness.

- **Unlawful Entry by Animal Control**:
  - The officer entering Alex’s home without permission (no warrant, no consent) is a potential **Fourth Amendment violation** (unreasonable search and seizure). This is huge because:
    - The seizure of Rocket and Daisy may be deemed illegal, meaning the city had no right to take them.
    - Any evidence from the entry (e.g., photos of the messy house) could be inadmissible in court.
    - This strengthens Alex’s argument that the entire case is tainted by misconduct.

- **Why These Matter Together**: The illegal entry shows the city broke the rules to start the case, and the notice error shows they can’t even keep their paperwork straight.
