### Analysis: The Clerical Error and Unlawful Entry Are Gold
You’re right to flag the Notice of Hearing error and the unlawful entry—both are *huge* leverage points, especially in a case that’s already a procedural mess. Here’s why they matter and how Alex (or you, if you take the case) can use them:

#### 1. **Clerical Error (Future Date on Notice)**
- **Why It’s a Big Deal**: A Notice of Hearing with a future date (November 29, 2025) for an event that already happened (February 3, 2025) is a *critical defect*. In legal land, proper notice is everything—courts need accurate documents to ensure due process. This error could:
  - Invalidate the notice, meaning Alex didn’t get proper notification of the hearing.
  - Undermine the city’s case, as it shows sloppy record-keeping, which could extend to other evidence (e.g., animal control’s reports).
  - Support a motion to dismiss or delay the case, since Alex can argue he was confused by the faulty notice.
- **Criminal vs. Civil Impact**: You mentioned criminal cases, and while this is civil, the “criminal vibe” (strict court rules, default judgments) makes the error even more potent. Courts hate procedural errors in any case, but especially when the city’s acting like this is a quasi-criminal matter. A judge might see this as a due process violation, especially if Alex missed the hearing due to the car theft and court’s refusal to reschedule.

#### 2. **Unlawful Entry**
- **Why It’s a Big Deal**: The animal control officer entering Alex’s home without permission (no warrant, no consent) is a potential Fourth Amendment violation. In civil or administrative animal cases, unlawful entry can:
  - Taint the evidence (e.g., photos of the messy house), making it inadmissible.
  - Justify dismissing the seizure, as the city’s case (dogs in unsafe conditions) relies on an illegal search.
  - Strengthen Alex’s argument that the city’s process was unfair from the start.
- **Link to Clerical Error**: The unlawful entry + the notice error paint a picture of a city that’s botching this case left and right. A judge might be more sympathetic to Alex if he shows the city’s been sloppy and unfair.
